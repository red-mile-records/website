        Stage 1 · Plan

        One room, one look, lit like the big shows

        Interview layout: armchair on the left of frame, couch in the centre and right, slat wall and the Serif 65 behind. The faces are the brightest thing in frame. The wall sits one to two stops under them, lifted by a low colour wash. Colour lives on the wall, never on skin.

        The brightness ladder

        How bright each layer reads on camera

        Key reads 300 lux at the face, the reference for every camera (f/2.8, ISO 400, 1/60 on the wide). A stop is double or half the light. Fill 1.5 stops under the key gives about a 3:1 ratio: shaped, not flat, and no hard shadow on the far cheek.

        What the biggest podcasts do

        Seven rules this set follows

        • Distance is depth. Every seat at least 3 ft off the slat wall so the wall can sit darker than faces.
        • Big, close, soft keys. 26 in lanterns about 4 ft from the face. Size relative to distance is what makes light soft.
        • Short-side keys. Each key sits on the side of the face away from that person's single camera. The far cheek gets shape, the near cheek gets fill.
        • One colour temperature on skin. Every light that touches a face runs at the camera's Kelvin, CRI 95 or better.
        • Separation. A rim on the hair and a wash on the wall keep dark hair from melting into the French Roast slats.
        • Colour in the background only. Show colour goes on the tubes and Govee bars. Faces stay neutral.
        • Practicals in frame, fixtures out. A warm lamp in shot sells the room. Stands and lanterns stay just outside every camera's frame.

          Frame checks

          Keep fixtures out of the wide

          • The wide (CAM1, 16mm) sees about 72° across. Its edges cross 3.4 ft in from each side wall at the seats, so both key stands sit outside them.
          • The rim strip hangs at 94 in. That leaves about 8 in to the 8.5 ft ceiling and keeps its bottom edge above the wide's top edge. Lower it only until it peeks into the wide, then back off 2 in.
          • The shelf takes the back-right corner, so the rim boom's base sits against the right wall just in front of it, behind the couch's right arm. Check it in the wide.
          • CAM2's background is the left wall, where the charcoal drape replaces the white curtain. Until it is up, keep the skirt on the K1 lantern's curtain side.
          • The wash tubes lie on the floor at the baseboard behind the armchair and couch. The furniture hides them from every camera.
          • Tape seat marks on the rug once faces meter right. Every show then starts from the same place.
          Mounting

          Ceiling approved, stands until the hardware is in

          The landlord has approved ceiling mounting in writing. Hang both lanterns and the rim from ceiling clamps with drop arms at the same positions and heights shown here. That frees the floor and takes every stand out of the wide. The hardware is on the shopping list to scout. Until it is up, use the stands.

          Sandbag every stand base. The 10×10 room has one exit, and a lantern on a tipped stand lands on a guest.

          Power: 3 to 4 COBs, 2 tubes and the RØDECaster share one circuit. Run lights on their own power bar and keep them off the audio power bar.

          Stage 4 · Camera

          Three bodies that cut together

          Every camera runs the same Kelvin as the face lights, the same shutter and the same exposure. All three run 1080p 29.97 for now into the RØDECaster Video, which switches and ISO-records each one. Recording 4K inside the two ZV-E10 IIs is the later upgrade for sharper vertical clips.

          Matching

          Make three cameras look like one

          1. Set every body to the same Kelvin as the face lights, G/M tint 0. Never use auto white balance.
          2. Put a grey card at each seat under show light. Expose so the card reads the same on all three cameras.
          3. Shoot 10 seconds of card on every body. In Resolve, match the RGB parade within 2 IRE.
          4. Save the a6000 correction as the PowerGrade RMR a6000 Match. Build a corrective LUT with make_lut.py (WB strength 0.6, white 232, black 2, saturation 1.06).
          5. Write the final ISO and match gap into the Rig Log.
          Framing

          Height, headroom, vertical safety

          • Lens height 48 to 50 in, about seated eye level, tilted down 2 to 3°. Raising the cameras was the Oct 28 call.
          • Head 8 to 10% from the top of frame. People inside the middle 80% of the width.
          • On CAM2 and CAM3, keep the face in the centre third so a 9:16 crop holds it. From 1080p that crop is about 608×1080 and soft; once the ZV-E10 IIs record 4K it is 1215×2160 and sharp.
          • Level the wide using the vertical slats as the check. Show the whole shelf on the right or frame it out. Half a shelf looks like a mistake.
          • CAM1 carries the teleprompter for solo and ad reads.
